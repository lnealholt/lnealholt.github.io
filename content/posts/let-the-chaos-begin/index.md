I have a print on my wall of my office from Amanda Oleander that is titled "The First Step is the Hardest".  She's not wrong.  I somehow always feel this pressure to have a first entry on a blog be this profound and meaningful artifact - as if that will somehow solidfy that this "thing" is worth doing.  Truth is - most often that just prevents me from ever actually following through with the "thing", so here we go...embracing the imperfection and instead just setting a 30 minute timer and see what comes out.  

I had this realization on Halloween this year as I was dressing up as witch and tying a stuffed rabbit to a broom to send my niece a video of me singing a ridiculous song that I wanted to document my life.  Being a millennial, I spent most of my high school and then college years uploading far too large of albums of terrible photos to Facebook and curating the perfect Myspace profile by adding broody songs based upon whatever hearbreak I was feeling at the time.  Then, as I got older and we had infuencers giving us constant "content", I suddenly felt like my own life wasn't interesting enough to post about anymore.  Once again, it felt like if I wasn't going to be the "best", then it wasn't worth doing at all (notice a theme?).  I somehow always just felt "cringe" because I was never going to live up to this idea of cool that I had in my head.  

Now, at 38, I'm starting to see it all a bit differently.  Here's the thing you realize as you get older.  Our lives are finite things and death comes for us all.  That sounds morbid (spoiler: I am - we'll get to all the dark stuff later), but eventually, the only thing remaining of us are the little bits and pieces that we've left behind - mementos of a life well lived (or at least we hope).  The problem is that those tend to fade over time.  We lose the pieces as generations pass and the stories get lost to time and the busyness of our days.  This somehow rings even truer for me.  As a woman who does not have children, I live each day with a certain type grief about the finality of what will remain of my story after I'm gone.  I'm a huge Hamilton fan, so of course as I'm writing this, I'm singing "Who lives, who dies, who tells your story?...".  The truth is that I want to be the one to tell it.  In all its chaotic imperfection.  

This is a space for anyone to follow along.
