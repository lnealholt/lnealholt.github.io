I have a problem.  I suffer from the affliction of getting something in my mind, and then immediately just hyper fixating on it until I possess more knowledge than I ever needed to have.  I can remember this as far back as 3rd grade when I became absolutely obsessed with the Titanic.  I'm pretty sure we had some unit in school that escalated into me checking out at least 15 books from the library on the subject.  I'm pretty sure this corresponded with the movie Titanic coming out in 1997, which had its own separate offshoot fixations, including Leonardo Dicaprio and Celine Dion. Yep - just me as an 9 year old listening to the Let's Talk About Love album on repeat. 

That same hyper fixation and general knowledge collection can occasionally (or my husband would say frequently) result in also convincing myself that I should just tackle projects that I have no business doing.  

The current project - a paver patio in my back yard/driveway.  After we had some concrete poured underneath our deck, they an area with some gravel on it and asked me if I wanted them to add soil and seed it.  I'm pretty sure they did not expect me to tell them "oh you can just leave it". 

{{< figure src="IMG_1575.jpeg" caption="Here's what we were working with to start...note the random concrete that I had to dig out." >}}

Because somehow in my mind, this was a perfect opportunity for me to add some whimsy to my backyard after seeing far too many Instagram reels of others adding patios to their homes.  Hours of research later, I was equipped with just enough knowledge to announce to my husband Brian that I was going to do some pavers in the space.  He did the thing that supportive husbands do...show me photos of a colleague's in-progress patio project (done by professionals) and pointing out how long it was taken with multiple crew members working on it.  I mean, he wasn't wrong.  But as expected, I was not dissuaded, and placed a Menards delivery order for pavers, sand, and some patio base panels.  A week later (because the delivery experience was awful), I had a pallet of pavers and sand sitting in my driveway. Then I got to make an extra trip to Dubuque to get the sand that Menards didn't deliver.

{{< figure src="IMG_1655.jpeg" caption="Don't mind the shadow that I couldn't be bothered to remove." >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_1664.jpeg" caption="These definitely didn't fall off the cart 2 minutes before this." >}}

I have this habit of just finding things I don't know how to do as an opportunity to collect a new skill and just accept that the first iteration won't be perfect.  Honestly, the idea that I don't know what I'm doing ends up somehow motivating me, as if this life is just one big opportunity to learn to do as many things as I can.  HOWEVER, lest I give you some ridiculous story that in thinking this, that it means all goes to plan every time,  this project was a lot more than I expected.

My dad passed away a few years ago, and as I was sitting there trying to figure out how to screed sand using whatever I could find in my garage, I very much felt like his daughter.  Despite his many flaws, the man was just always able to figure things out.  His scale  was always a bit larger considering he just decided one day he was going to build my parents' house himself.  My mom still lives in that house to this day.  Unfortunately, my skill level isn't quite as robust as his.  I feel like there were multiple times during that I wished I had asked more questions of my dad when he was still around.  When I was a kid, I was always notorious for trying to get out of helping my dad.  Much of that was his tendency to have zero patience and you were absolutely guaranteed to get yelled at, but there have been so many times where I thought "if only I had paid attention".  Grief if always funny like that.  I find it so rarely shows up in the ways you expect.  It's little moments of realizing that some things will always just remain unsaid or unanswered.  

Anyway...
{{< figure src="IMG_1662.jpeg" caption="This actually powered the project." >}}


Halfway through, when I absolutely could not get the sand to be level on one side, I regretted everything.  Would the correct thing have been to continue until it was actually level? Sure.  Is that what I did? Nope. Eventually, I just accepted that I was losing steam and decided that the best way to continue my motivation was just to accept the imperfection and just put down the dang pavers and move on before I ended up with a pile of unfinished nonsense that I had to look at for the rest of the winter. 

{{< figure src="IMG_1656.jpeg" caption="Using orange stakes so it looks like I actually measured this properly. If I could go back to this moment, I would pull up the plastic and start immediately digging out the far left corner to save myself several hours of heartache later" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_1677.jpeg" caption= "Pile of sand and rigged up attempt to actual level it using 3/4 inch pipes I bought after wondering around Lowe's." >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_1678.jpeg" caption="Occasionally got the leveling right - so I obviously had to send a photo to my husband immediately." >}}

After finishing the pavers and getting them pseudo decent to look at, I realized I had a huge gap between the patio and the asphalt that is in major need of repair.  So, new quest activated.  Driveway connection with a ramp.  I had no idea what I was doing, so I obviously started researching and ended up buying 6 buckets of Aquaphalt to make it look like there was some intention to connect it to the existing asphalt that barely resembles asphalt.  Kudos to the random patching as well from after we had our concrete poured under the deck. 

{{< figure src="IMG_1710.jpeg" caption="Mind the gap." >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_1712.jpeg" caption= "Surprisingly easy enough to use.  It apparently just binds with water, and doesn't smell the way regular asphalt does because it doesn't contain petroleum.  You just put it on, use a hose to wet it down, then tamp it. Even I couldn't mess it up." >}}

What I ended up with was an imperfect patio that I'm obsessed with. Does it have random spots that definitely aren't level? Sure.  I also had to dig out way too much sod afterward to even up the landscaping, which took me about 5 times longer than I expected, but now it looks like a yard that we put some thought into.  Whimsical details to come in the spring.  For now, I'm bringing my projects inside.  Tiling of the bathroom yet to come.  It might be a disaster.  I'll still do it anyway.
